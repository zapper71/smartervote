# Huntsville issue taxonomy — DRAFT for Andreas to edit

**Status:** draft, not loaded into the database yet.
**Your call per Q06:** I draft, you edit.
**Why it matters:** this list *is* the site's editorial position. Every candidate gets described against these same issues, in this same order. Leave one out and you've decided it doesn't matter. Add a loaded one and you've tilted the whole comparison. Nothing else on the site deserves more of your attention.

---

## How to edit this

- **Delete freely.** Ten issues is already a lot for a voter to read across six candidates. Eight is probably better.
- **Rewrite the voter question.** That's the text people actually see as the section heading. It should sound like something a neighbour would ask, not like a policy paper.
- **Watch for loaded framing.** "Fixing the housing crisis" presumes a conclusion. "Housing" doesn't.
- **Add anything local I've missed.** I don't live there. You do.

---

## The honest caveat

I drafted these from general knowledge of Ontario municipal government and of Muskoka as a region — cottage-country growth pressure, seasonal population, lake health, a two-tier council structure. **I have not yet read the ten candidate websites.**

That matters. A taxonomy built from the outside risks asking about issues nobody in Huntsville is actually arguing about, while missing the one thing every candidate's front page mentions.

**Recommended sequence:** you do a first pass on this list now, I read all ten candidate sites, then we revise it together before anything gets loaded. That way the final taxonomy reflects the actual campaign rather than my assumptions about it.

## Draft issues

### 1. Housing and affordability
**Voter question:** *Where are people supposed to live?*
Attainable and affordable housing, rental supply, seasonal-vs-permanent residents, whether people who work in Huntsville can afford to live in Huntsville.

### 2. Short-term rentals
**Voter question:** *How should short-term rentals be handled?*
Licensing, enforcement, caps, the tension between tourism income and neighbourhood housing supply. *Flagging this as its own issue rather than folding it into housing — in cottage country it's usually a live, distinct fight. Tell me if that's wrong for Huntsville.*

### 3. Growth and development
**Voter question:** *How much should Huntsville grow, and where?*
The Official Plan, subdivision approvals, density, downtown vs outlying wards, protecting rural character.

### 4. Property taxes and town spending
**Voter question:** *What happens to my tax bill?*
The municipal levy, the District of Muskoka portion, where money gets spent, what a candidate would cut or fund.

### 5. Roads and infrastructure
**Voter question:** *Will the roads, water and sewers get fixed?*
Capital renewal backlog, road maintenance, water and wastewater capacity, winter maintenance.

### 6. Lakes, water quality and environment
**Voter question:** *Who's protecting the lakes?*
Shoreline development, water quality monitoring, climate adaptation, natural heritage.

### 7. Healthcare access
**Voter question:** *Can I see a doctor?*
Physician and nurse-practitioner recruitment, the hospital, emergency services, seniors' care. *Largely provincial jurisdiction — but municipalities do recruit and incentivise, and voters raise it regardless. Worth including precisely because the honest answer involves explaining what council can and can't do.*

### 8. Economic development and downtown
**Voter question:** *How does the local economy get stronger?*
Business attraction and retention, downtown vitality, tourism, employment lands, broadband.

### 9. Recreation, arts and community services
**Voter question:** *What does the town provide for residents?*
Facilities, trails, the Algonquin Theatre, library, programming, user fees.

### 10. How council works
**Voter question:** *How would this person actually govern?*
Transparency, public consultation, the Town/District two-tier relationship, decision-making style, accountability. *This is where non-partisan municipal races are genuinely decided — voters are picking temperament and judgment, not a platform. Often the most useful section on the page.*

---

## Two I deliberately left out

**Policing and community safety** — Huntsville is OPP-served, so council's role is limited to the contract and budget. Include it only if candidates are actually raising it.

**Climate change as a standalone issue** — folded into #6 instead. As its own heading it tends to sort candidates into partisan camps before they've said anything, which cuts against the whole point of a non-partisan comparison. Worth a conversation if you disagree.

## Rules to apply once the list is final

1. **Every candidate gets every issue.** No exceptions, no shortened profiles for candidates with less material.
2. **Where there's no public position, say so explicitly** — "No public position found as of [date]" — and link the correction form. Never leave a blank; a blank reads as an editorial judgment.
3. **Same order on every page**, so comparison is possible at a glance.
4. **Every non-empty position carries a quote, a source link and a date.** The database constraint enforces this; don't work around it.
5. **Issue order is not a ranking.** Say that on the methodology page before someone assumes otherwise.
