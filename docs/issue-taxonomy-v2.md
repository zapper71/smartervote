## Where I was wrong

**Short-term rentals: I had this as issue #2. It appears on ZERO of the six platforms.**

I assumed it because it's a live fight in a lot of cottage country. In Huntsville, on this evidence, nobody is campaigning on it. Leaving it in would have asked six candidates to answer a question none of them raised, made the site feel imported rather than local, and crowded out something that matters more. **Cutting it.**

**I badly underweighted the Town–District relationship.** Three of six raise it directly, and pointedly: Caswell wants "a stronger voice at the District table" and less duplication, Stone wants to "reduce the size and influence of District Planning," Maw wants to bridge "taxpayers, local residents, and regional governance." In a two-tier municipality this turns out to be a real dividing line, not a technicality. It deserves its own heading.

**I missed the hospital entirely.** It's the single most concrete fiscal commitment in this election. Caswell names a figure — a **$10 million local share** — and proposes a dedicated Huntsville Community Hospital Fund. Armour lists healthcare advocacy. Stone wants it "to move forward quickly." This is the closest thing to a defining issue the race has.

**I missed things only locals would know.** The **Dark Sky bylaw** (Stone championed it, Koop references dark skies), **Brendale Square** revitalisation, **Chaffey Township Road** reconstruction, rural **cell service**, and **doctor recruitment** (Stone and Scott Morrison ran a program that brought in 8 doctors). None of these would have occurred to me from outside.

**My "how council works" instinct was right, and then some.** All six lead with listening, transparency and communication. In a non-partisan race this is what voters are actually choosing between.

## The revised list — 9 issues

Ordered by how many of the six raise them. **Order is not a ranking of importance** and the site must say so.

### 1. Taxes, spending and value for money
**Voter question:** *What happens to my tax bill, and where does the money go?*
**Raised by:** 6 of 6.
Municipal levy, the District portion, efficiencies, waste, what they'd cut or fund. Armour wants "a comprehensive financial and governance review." Stone voted against a District Council salary increase. Maw wrote a whole post explaining the three-layer tax bill.

### 2. Roads, infrastructure and the basics
**Voter question:** *Will the things I use every day actually get fixed?*
**Raised by:** 6 of 6.
Roads, sidewalks, bridges, snow removal, water and wastewater, safe intersections. Specifics that came up: Chaffey Township Road reconstruction, winter sidewalk clearing, safe walking routes to school, replacing the aging Operations Centre, and — in Brunel — reliable cell service.

### 3. How council works: transparency, listening and accountability
**Voter question:** *How would this person actually govern?*
**Raised by:** 6 of 6.
Public consultation, communication, openness about decisions. Chiaramonte proposes publishing why she voted as she did. Koop: "you may not always get the answer you hoped for, but you should always feel heard." This is where a non-partisan race is genuinely decided.

### 4. Growth, development and community character
**Voter question:** *How much should Huntsville grow, and where?*
**Raised by:** 5 of 6.
The Official Plan, the height and density plan, intensification versus rural character, whether infrastructure keeps pace. Framed by nearly everyone as a balance rather than a yes/no.

### 5. Housing and affordability
**Voter question:** *Can people who work here afford to live here?*
**Raised by:** 4 of 6.
Purpose-built rental, attainable housing, secondary dwelling units, seniors on fixed incomes, young people priced out. Armour proposes reducing or eliminating development charges on qualifying rental projects — the most specific housing lever anyone names.

### 6. Healthcare and the new hospital
**Voter question:** *Can I see a doctor, and how will we pay for the new hospital?*
**Raised by:** 3 of 6, but it is the most concrete money question in the election.
The $10 million local share. Doctor and nurse-practitioner recruitment. Whether the Town sets funds aside now or scrambles later.
*Note for the page: much of healthcare is provincial. Say what council actually controls — the local share, recruitment incentives, advocacy — rather than implying more.*

### 7. Environment, lakes and dark skies
**Voter question:** *Who's protecting the lakes and the night sky?*
**Raised by:** 4 of 6.
Shoreline and water quality, forests and natural heritage, environmental impact studies in planning decisions, and the Dark Sky outdoor lighting bylaw — a distinctly Muskoka issue with a real enforcement record.

### 8. Huntsville's place at the District table
**Voter question:** *Is the two-tier system working for Huntsville, or costing us?*
**Raised by:** 3 of 6, and pointedly.
Duplication between Town and District, District planning authority, whether Huntsville leads or reacts. A genuine dividing line.
*Note for the page: this needs a plain-language explainer of what the District does. Most voters have never had it explained.*

### 9. Local economy, jobs and downtown
**Voter question:** *Will there be decent work here, and a downtown worth walking to?*
**Raised by:** 3 of 6.
Business attraction and retention, downtown vitality, Brendale Square, tourism. Maw is blunt about the underlying problem: seasonal, part-time, low-paying work alongside an influx of outside wealth driving up costs.

---

### 10. Parks, trails and active transportation ✅ ADDED
**Voter question:** *Can you get around safely without a car, and are the parks and trails looked after?*
**Raised by:** 3 of 6.
The Parks and Trails Master Plan, trail connections between neighbourhoods, cycling and pedestrian safety, year-round accessibility. Stone chairs the Active Transportation committee, Caswell names trails repeatedly, Chiaramonte wants ward input into the Master Plan.

## The two I proposed that were NOT included

**Community safety and emergency services** (2 of 6). Fire, emergency services, winter maintenance. Caswell treats it as a pillar; for most others it folds into "the basics" (issue 2). Left out.

**Social services, food security and homelessness** (0 of 6 platforms — but look where it came from). Three of the top items residents themselves added to Chiaramonte's public list are about The Table Soup Kitchen Foundation, which she says feeds over 9% of Huntsville's population, and a proposed 34-bed emergency shelter. **Not one candidate platform mentions it.** That gap is itself informative — a question residents are raising that no campaign is answering.

Left out for V1. The argument for including it was that the gap is the story; the argument against is that it would show "no public position found" for nearly every candidate, which reads as stark and invites the accusation that the site chose an issue to embarrass people with. Worth revisiting after launch, and a good candidate for a post-election piece.

## Two operational facts worth having

**PIN letters are mailed starting 30 September.** That's when this becomes real for voters — and it's two weeks before your launch date. Worth considering whether launching a few days earlier catches people at the moment they start paying attention.

**There is an Election Help Centre** at Partner's Hall, Town Hall, 37 Main St E, open from 14 October for people without a computer or phone. Voting is internet and telephone only — no polling stations. The site should say this clearly; it's the single most confusing thing about this election for anyone expecting to show up somewhere and mark a ballot.
